# Focused Implementation Repair

Read this reference when verification exposes an in-scope defect or a bounded review returns causally supported findings. A repair is bounded by its objective, scope, authority, and acceptance evidence, not by a default number of attempts.

1. Establish the current failure and its connection to the authorized acceptance baseline. Admit it only when evidence shows it blocks the authorized outcome or necessary correctness or safety within the chosen design and authority; thematic relevance, a red test, an accepted label, or a small diff does not establish that by itself. If the selected mechanism is causing the repair stream, compare simplifying or removing it with further hardening before treating each resulting defect as a new goal. A necessary repair continues, and this comparison does not stop the authorized task. The implementing parent adjudicates material review candidates; reviewer preference is not repair authority. A worker can diagnose and repair ordinary local failures within its existing brief without returning every red-green iteration.
2. Diagnose the smallest supported cause. Combine related accepted findings when they share that cause, preserve unrelated user changes, and modify only authorized surfaces. For delegated follow-up, prefer the original valid worker through the host's supported continuation path; use [Delegated Execution](delegated-execution.md) for the incremental brief, current-state checks, and capability failures. Parent authorization of the repair is distinct from performing it.
3. Apply the same-slice cut from Implement And Verify and rerun the affected checks and declared verification. Do not weaken a required outcome to fit the repair. An authorized best-effort secondary adaptation with evidence is not oracle weakening; relabeling a required result as secondary or deleting its oracle is. Within existing authority, correct or remove mistaken or over-specified assertions using the user's requirements and actual consumer evidence, then verify the required behavior. A self-invented test is not a new requirement; review remains conditional under the owning workflow.
4. Judge the current version using evidence that still covers its actual candidate. If a defect remains and evidence supports another in-scope step, continue diagnosis and repair. A second or later failure is not itself a stopping condition. Local check success does not settle parent acceptance or prove the integrated result.
5. Request targeted rereview only when the repair changes evidence relied on by the prior review, an independent question remains, or an applicable rule requires it. Bound that invocation to the repair, affected boundaries, and unresolved findings. Reuse still-valid dispositions; do not reopen them without new evidence. A new regression cannot hide behind an old pass.
6. Complete when the authorized objective, required checks, and accepted findings are satisfied. Disclosed authorized secondary tradeoffs may remain. With no new change, failure, or unresolved risk, do not repeat verification or review for its own sake.

Stop for the actual blocker:

- Contradictory acceptance, invalid design premises, or a required scope/dependency change: preserve evidence and return the appropriate `replan` or `redesign` decision, without rewriting the approved baseline. Local technical difficulty, one failed attempt, or an authorized secondary omission is not automatic `replan` or `redesign`.
- Missing authority, input, or environment capability: return `needs-authority` or `blocked` with the exact gap. Independently completable authorized work may proceed, but the whole task is not a pass. After the user answers the blocking question, resume the original authorized repair rather than ending on confirmation.
- Repeated diagnosis with no new evidence and no reasonable in-scope path: return `non-convergent`, explaining excluded paths and missing evidence, not an attempt count. Ordinary failure or unknown feasibility is not itself proof that a required result is unsupported.
- User cancellation or an explicit invocation time, call, or resource budget: obey the limit and report `blocked` with current evidence and remaining work. Budget exhaustion is neither success nor proof of an impossible objective.

The reviewer remains read-only and never owns repair or continuation. Unrelated pre-existing debt, unrelated improvements, future-phase, speculative, and plan-expanding observations do not enter the repair. A pre-existing caller or schema mismatch is not excluded when evidence shows it blocks the authorized delivery under the necessity, scope, design, and authority test above. Progress and evidence may be recorded; goals, non-goals, authority, dependencies, and acceptance may not be silently changed. If a secondary adaptation would make a later production action exceed the approved boundary, pause that affected action and continue independent authorized repair.
